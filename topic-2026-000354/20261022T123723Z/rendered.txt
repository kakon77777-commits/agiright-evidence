Requirements for qualified independent external evaluators of General-Purpose AI models
EVENT REPORT
Publication 21 October 2026

The AI Office presents a report on its workshop on the requirements for the qualifications and independence of external model evaluators of general-purpose AI (GPAI) models with systemic risk.

During the online workshop on 15 July 2026, the AI Office presented a proposal for these requirements. Experts on AI model evaluation from 19 organisations took part in this meeting.

Overall, 20 organisations were selected to participate following a public call for expressions of interest. The selection criteria covered demonstrated experience in conducting high-quality evaluations of GPAI models and their systemic risks, as well as the quality of thought leadership in AI governance, particularly in systemic risk evaluations.

The involvement of external evaluators is a key element for assessing and mitigating the systemic risks that may stem from GPAI models with systemic risk – as set out in Article 55(1)(b) of the AI Act and specified in the GPAI Code of Practice.

This workshop addressed the AI Office's objective of developing and communicating (parts of) a system for scoring external evaluators on qualification and independence criteria. Assessing whether providers of GPAI models with systemic risk comply with the AI Act is one of the AI Office’s tasks. In this context, ensuring that external evaluators are adequately qualified and independent is crucial for the reliability of model evaluations as credible input towards assessing and mitigating systemic risks. In particular, evaluators should bring domain expertise and the necessary evaluation skills for systemic risk assessment, as well as being free from the provider’s managerial, financial or operational influence.

The attendees of the workshop provided highly valuable input, including suggesting clarifications on the proposed scoring system, as well as on criteria for scoring financial and management independence.

The proposal presented by the AI Office received positive feedback, but also some questions, clarifications, and concerns. The AI Office addressed the raised points both orally during the workshop and through a written report (that you can download below). This report expresses the current views of the AI Office, which may be subject to change as the field of AI evaluations develops.
