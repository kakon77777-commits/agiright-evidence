UT Research Foundation files patent infringement lawsuit against AI giant Anthropic

Logan West, Staff Writer
August 23, 2026
Logan West
Laptop displays Claude, Anthropic’s Ai software.

UT chemistry department to open new building in 2029

As UT administrators strive to recruit new rockstar researchers, three of the university’s past and present faculty members are fighting to reclaim their decade-old project.

In mid-July, the University of Tennessee Research Foundation filed the first ever patent infringement lawsuit against Anthropic, an artificial intelligence company known for creating the popular language model Claude.

The court filings

The lawsuit alleges that the AI enterprise utilized machine-learning technology created by UT researchers J. Douglas Birdwell, Mark E. Dean and Catherine Schuman, infringing upon two patents the researchers filed in 2014.

The patents-in-suit are the property of the UTRF, a nonprofit that supports university research efforts. UTRF is responsible for protecting UT intellectual property and promoting its commercialization. 

The patents cover neural network technology, a machine learning model inspired by the structure and functions of the human brain.

UTRF claims Anthropic used this patented technology in Claude Code and basic software without authorization, but it is unclear how the company gained access to the technology.

According to the complaint, Anthropic has not been in connection or communication with the organization to obtain permission for use. The complaint says the AI giant “has been no friend to the developers and owners of intellectual property rights.”

UTRF is seeking damages stemming from Anthropic’s infringement and any other relief that might be entitled to them.

Past legal disputes and public concern

Anthropic is no stranger to legal battles. While this lawsuit is the first regarding patent infringement, the enterprise recently settled a $1.5 billion copyright lawsuit over pirated books used to train its AI models.

While patent infringement and piracy are certainly cause for concern, many also criticize the potential safety concerns of the company. 

President Donald Trump took to Truth Social in February to direct all federal agencies to cease use of Anthropic’s products, stating they were a major security threat.   

“We don’t need it, we don’t want it, and will not do business with them again,” Trump said.

On Aug. 10, a group of House Democrats sought to summon leaders of Anthropic and other major AI companies to testify before the U.S. Congress on the topic of public safety and privacy. 

After many instances of hacking from rogue AI agents, lawmakers on both sides have called for stronger legislation and accountability from creators, making this a bipartisan issue.

Why this matters for UT

Large federal agencies, private entities and educational institutions including UT have had to adapt to the widespread usage of AI. 

If the allegations are proven correct, UT unknowingly played a critical role in creating Claude, an AI chatbot with over 18 million monthly active users.

UTRF and Anthropic have not yet responded to a request for comment.
