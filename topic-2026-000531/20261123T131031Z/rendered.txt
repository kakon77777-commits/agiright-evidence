NGA’s Bipartisan ‘Governors Council to Secure American AI Dominance’ issues policy recommendations to ensure safety, promote innovation, clarify state/federal roles

WASHINGTON, D.C. – The National Governors Association (NGA) today announced new principles for governance of AI at state and federal levels. Developed through NGA’s bipartisan Governors Council to Secure American AI Dominance — led by NGA Chair Maryland Gov. Wes Moore, NGA Vice Chair Indiana Gov. Mike Braun, North Carolina Gov. Josh Stein and Utah Gov. Spencer Cox — the principles are designed to help states build a shared national approach, promote consistency across state frameworks, and inform the incoming Congress as it develops an AI framework consistent with federalism principles.

“Governors from across the country agree that AI poses both opportunities and risks,” said Gov. Wes Moore. “We agree that humans must stay in control, and we must protect our people – especially workers, consumers and our kids. Governors will continue to focus both on creating jobs and making sure that workers have a voice in deployment of new technology.”

“States are moving quickly to ensure our workforces and state governments are AI-ready,” said Gov. Mike Braun. “Artificial intelligence has great potential to generate economic growth, create more high-quality jobs, increase wages and make government services more efficient. Both the federal government and the states have a responsibility to promote innovation and protect the public from harm, and this framework strikes the right balance to ensure both succeed.”

“Governors in both parties believe states have a critical role in responsible AI advancement, and we are calling on Congress to work with us to develop a federal framework that strengthens American competitiveness while protecting the public from harm,” said Gov. Spencer Cox. “We don’t have to choose between innovation and safety. Many states have already enacted thoughtful policies that foster AI adoption and advancement while protecting children and consumers. Governors are ready to work with Congress and the White House on a national AI strategy.”

“Artificial intelligence presents both opportunities and risks that are too big to ignore,” said Governor Josh Stein. “Governors are calling on the federal government to act to protect the public, support innovation, and preserve appropriate state authority. As states implement policies to prepare our workforce for opportunity, protect the public from harm, and transform how government can deliver for people, these bipartisan principles can help build the trust needed for innovation to thrive.” 

Principles released today include the following topline priorities:

Advance innovation AND protect the public: AI policies should strengthen and ensure dominance of American technological leadership and economic opportunity while protecting public safety, civil rights, and individual privacy. The United States will not win the AI technological race if the public does not have faith in its safety and that it will benefit the people.
Support workers: States should address the challenges that workers may face on the job during the rapid acceleration of AI. Job creation must be a core priority as AI is rolled out across the economy, as should protecting workers from potential harms and developing the program infrastructure needed to support workers who face transition.
Establish clear and complementary federal and state roles: The federal government should address national and international risks as AI is developed, while states retain the flexibility to protect people and govern AI use within their responsibilities as AI is deployed under the federal system. 
Match safeguards to credible evidence-based risks: AI requirements should use clear, technically informed definitions and proportionate safeguards, with regular review as technological capabilities evolve.  
Promote consistency while preserving effective protections: Federal and state approaches should reduce unnecessary duplication and conflicting requirements wherever possible. 
Encourage competition and development: Federal and state laws and regulations should encourage healthy competition and development and not favor larger companies or create unnecessary impediments to smaller companies entering the market. 

“Governors are on the frontlines of AI development in the states, implementing policies to advance American leadership and create jobs while guarding against safety risks and workforce disruption,” said NGA CEO Brandon Tatum. “It’s clearly time for a national AI approach, but no federal AI policy will be effective without governors’ input. Governors have an important role in ensuring that any federal framework for artificial intelligence preserves appropriate state authority. Governors formed this bipartisan council to spur action and ensure federal-state coordination. Technology as transformative as AI requires a comprehensive, coordinated strategy, and governors look forward to engaging with Congress, the White House and industry leaders in this vital work.”
