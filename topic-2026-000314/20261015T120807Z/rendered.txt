Attorney General Griffin Sues OpenAI for Misleading Arkansans over Safety of ChatGPT
October 14, 2026
Griffin: ‘OpenAI Assures Us That ChatGPT Is Safe. The Consequences Of That Assurance Are Lives Lost And People Harmed’

LITTLE ROCK – Attorney General Griffin issued the following statement after filing a lawsuit against tech giant OpenAI:

“We’re in the midst of a technological revolution. Whether you call it artificial intelligence or super intelligence, the bottom line is that things are rapidly changing. But one thing that has not changed and will not change is Arkansans’ right to receive truthful information about the products and services they use.

“That’s why earlier today I filed a lawsuit against OpenAI, the company that owns the platform ChatGPT. Our complaint outlines numerous violations of Arkansas law, but they all boil down to a simple fact: OpenAI represented to Arkansans that ChatGPT was safe, but they knew ChatGPT had significant risks and limitations. They chose to hide that knowledge from consumers.

“Our complaint alleges that consumers, including minors, have used ChatGPT to acquire instructions for self-harm, including suicide. It also describes instances where users were able to bypass OpenAI’s safety measures and generate responses the company said its safeguards were designed to prevent. Those examples raise serious questions about whether consumers were given an accurate picture of the product’s safety protections.

“OpenAI assures us that ChatGPT is safe. The consequences of that assurance are lives lost and people harmed.”

Arkansas’s lawsuit against OpenAI includes three causes of action:
• Violations of the Arkansas Deceptive Trade Practices Act (ADTPA), including knowingly making false representations to consumers; unconscionable, false, or deceptive practices; deception and fraud in interactions with consumers; and omissions of material facts with the intent for consumers to rely upon them
• Children’s and Teens’ Online Privacy Protection Act (CTOPPA) violations, including knowingly collecting personal information from children and teens; knowingly retaining the personal information of a child or teen for longer than is reasonably necessary; and failing to provide notice and gain consent for the collection of data and information
• Common Law claims, including negligence; strict liability – design defect; strict liability – failure to warn; unjust enrichment; and public nuisance

Arkansas’s lawsuit seeks relief through:
• A declaration that OpenAI violated the ADTPA and CTOPPA and an injunction from further violations, including an injunction from collecting the data of minors
• A requirement that OpenAI provide clear and conspicuous disclosures of the material risks of ChatGPT
• Restitution to consumers who have been affected
• Civil penalties
• Injunction from further advanced AI testing and creating models capable of recursive self-improvement until adequate safeguards are in place
