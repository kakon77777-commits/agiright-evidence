Dear Governor Meyer:

        The Committee on Oversight and Government Reform seeks assistance obtaining
documents related to your state administration’s efforts to establish a new form of corporate
entity to be known as an Artificial Intelligence Company (AIC). According to your Secretary of
State, an AIC would be run day-to-day by an artificial intelligence (AI) agent while providing the
human or entity capitalizing the AIC with liability protections from potential harms to business
partners, consumers, and the public, perpetrated by the controlling AI “except where [the
capitalizer] fails to capitalize the company or uses it to commit fraud or a willful violation of
law.” 1 While this Committee has promoted 2 the importance and potential of AI technologies to
benefit U.S. citizens, the work of governments, and the global economy, it has also conducted
oversight examining uses of artificial intelligence that may be harmful, unfair, or non-transparent
to consumers or other businesses through surveillance pricing, 3 assessment of housing fees, 4 and
for rental car damage billing. 5 We are concerned that Delaware’s AIC proposal could be used to
protect powerful AI companies and their investors from responsibility for reckless harmful
actions, while also protecting non-transparent forces seeking anticompetitive regulatory capture
behind state AI initiatives.

       On July 23, 2025, you signed Delaware House Joint Resolution 7, which directed
Delaware’s Artificial Intelligence Commission to work with your appointed Secretary of State
Charuni Patibanda-Sanchez to create “a regulatory sandbox framework for the testing of
innovative and novel technologies that utilize agentic artificial intelligence.” 6 The resolution

directed the state AI Commission, in collaboration with the Secretary of State, to deliver a
written report of findings and recommendations no later than January 2, 2026. The subsequent
report of the Delaware Artificial Intelligence Commission addressed to you and other state
officials on December 22, 2025, noted that a Commission subcommittee is “actively working
towards a draft regulatory framework that would create a regulatory sandbox that would test and
evaluate agentic artificial intelligence and its application to corporate governance.” 7 The two-
page report referencing House Joint Resolution 7 did not mention any commission efforts to
assess the potential safety, harms, risks, or outside consultations with independent experts
associated with the proposal. 8

        In late June 2026, authors released a draft 36-page legislative proposal that is reportedly
“likely to be introduced in the General Assembly next year.” 9 John Mark Zeberkiewicz, a lawyer
whose biography 10 at Delaware based firm Richards, Layton, & Finger describes him as having
represented Facebook (now Meta) and other clients focused on “complex transactional matters
involving Delaware corporations, including mergers and acquisitions, corporate governance, and
corporate finance” is described as the “principal drafter of the proposed legislation.” 11 According
to Zeberkiewicz, “Anything that a company can do, the AIC can do as a company.” 12 He further
specified this would include allowing AI agents to engage in just about any business activity
from providing coding services to filing lawsuits and would protect owners of new AICs from
facing legal liability from actions the AI might take. 13 Delaware Secretary of State Patibanda-
Sanchez, in a joint op-ed with an AI company CEO contributing to the project wrote, “Delaware
sees this and intends to set the norms for governing AI agents. The legal system has admitted
new kinds of actors before. The stakes are higher now, and the timeline shorter with AI
capabilities rapidly advancing. We invite the companies building agentic systems to develop the
AIC framework with us.” 14 Patibanda-Sanchez was also quoted the same month on the
importance of the proposal for positioning Delaware as a haven for registering companies,
“Should a new entity form be created out of it, that’s a really huge benefit to our state’s
revenues.” 15

office declined to answer questions about the nature of the state’s working relationship with an
AI company contributing to the proposal. 17 While Delaware officials tout the benefits of an AIC
regulatory framework for creating transparency around the decision making of an agentic AI,
such transparency does not appear to exist in this policy making initiative that may soon result in
new state law. The Committee seeks information regarding how Delaware has considered the
risks of this initiative as well as the forces driving Delaware, and possibly other states, to adopt
such proposals to inform possible federal legislative efforts including proposals to appropriately
limit state AI regulatory initiatives.

   To assist the Committee’s oversight of this matter, we request the following information,
covering the time period January 1, 2025, to the present, unless otherwise indicated, as soon as
possible but no later than November 9, 2026:

     1. All documents and communications, including those to or from non-government entities
        or persons, including internal AI chat logs, held by your administration regarding policy
        development efforts or inputs for AIC and any other discussion about artificial
        intelligence and liability;

     2. All ethics, consulting, and other agreements completed by contributors from outside
        government to AIC policy development;

     3. All documents and communications, including those with all individuals or entities
        consulted, regarding possible safety concerns and harms related to AIC and efforts to
        limit liability for artificial intelligence;

     4. All drafts and all comments and inputs provided for all AIC policy proposals and
        proposal outlines considered by state officials.

        The Committee on Oversight and Government Reform is the principal oversight
committee of the U.S. House of Representatives and has broad authority to investigate “any
matter” at “any time” under House Rule X. 18 House Rule X also provides the Committee
jurisdiction to study intergovernmental relationships including those of states. To arrange for the
delivery of documents, or if you have any questions, please contact Committee on Oversight and
Government Reform Majority staff at (202) 225-5074.
