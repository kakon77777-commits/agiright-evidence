SUBJECT:       Implementing the National Policy for Nucleic Acid
               Synthesis Screening in Accordance with Executive Order
               14292

Biotechnology is central to American medicine, research, and
industrial competitiveness; nucleic acid synthesis is one of the core
technologies underpinning modern biological innovation. The United
States stands at the threshold of a profound transformation driven by
the convergence of super intelligence (SI) and biology. In an SI­
enabled future, biotechnology innovation and manufacturing
increasingly rely on nucleic acid synthesis to translate discoveries
into the physical world. If misused, however, synthetic nucleic acids
can also supply the raw materials for biological threats.

We must continue to promote biotechnology innovation without
jeopardizing our Nation's security, strength, or prosperity. Existing
Federal guidance has relied on self-attestation rather than
independent verification, forcing responsible American companies to
compete against firms that bypass biosecurity safeguards for economic
gain. Executive Order 14292 (on Improving the Safety and Security of
Biological Research) calls for a national policy to replace the 2024
Framework for Nucleic Acid Synthesis Screening with a commonsense
approach that closes the gaps malicious actors exploit.

Attached is the 2026 National Policy for Nucleic Acid Synthesis
Screening ("Policy"). It requires all recipients of Federal life
sciences funding to procure synthetic nucleic acids and benchtop
synthesis equipment exclusively from certified suppliers that verify
the legitimacy of customers and sequences ordered. The Policy replaces
the 2024 Framework for Nucleic Acid Synthesis Screening and, on full
implementation, the 2023 HHS Screening Framework Guidance. It supports
the 2026 U.S. Government Policy for Stopping High-Risk Life Sciences
Research by limiting a source of material inputs for dangerous gain of
function work, without stifling biological innovation.

The Policy conditions Federal funding on compliance with verifiable
standards and establishes a dynamic, tech-enabled screening layer that
keeps pace with evolving risks, including those from SI uplift. Within
90 days, the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST),
working with other departments and agencies and the private sector,
will issue standards and test and evaluation protocols for screening
and benchtop equipment used in nucleic acid synthesis. NIST will
subsequently begin verifying screeners and maintaining a list of those
approved, and the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) will
publish the first list of adherent suppliers within 160 days. All
customers that receive Federal funding must procure all synthetic
nucleic acids from certified suppliers within 250 days, and all
benchtop synthesis equipment within 340 days.

Frontier SI capabilities will unlock new opportunities for scientific
discovery and transform how our Nation defends against biological
threats. Through this Policy, the Administration is building a dynamic
biosecurity defense centered on strong controls over the materials
that enable synthetic biology misuse. Powered by SI, screening will
shift from list-based to risk-based, creating a flexible framework
that keeps pace with rapid technological advancement. These controls,
in turn, will ensure the United States can continue extending the most
powerful SI capabilities to our biotechnology innovators with
confidence.

The Policy will position the United States to lead a new era of SI-
enabled biotechnology innovation while setting the global standard for
security. The future of biotechnology is being built in the United
States, the home of the researchers, institutions, and companies that
pioneered the modern tools of biology, from gene editing to high-
throughput sequencing. Today, we are at the forefront of SI-enabled
biological science, and American companies are the leading suppliers
of synthetic nucleic acids and lab-scale DNA synthesis equipment to
researchers around the world. By ensuring that providers of synthetic
nucleic acid sequences implement comprehensive, scalable, and
verifiable screening mechanisms, we will minimize the risk of misuse
and share the benefits of secure biotechnology innovation with
Americans and the world.

Purpose
Nucleic acid synthesis is a transformative technology for medicine, research, and the
American bioeconomy. However, if used maliciously, it can threaten the Nation’s economic
and national security. For too long, American biotechnology leaders and other responsible
innovators have had to compete against firms that bypass biosecurity safeguards for
economic gain. To reverse these incentives, the United States will advance verifiable
standards that turn responsible biosecurity practices into a competitive advantage in the
global market.
Pursuant to Executive Order (EO) 14292, “Improving the Safety and Security of Biological
Research,” the 2026 Nucleic Acid Synthesis Screening Policy (hereafter “Policy”) establishes
comprehensive, scalable, and verifiable synthetic nucleic acid procurement screening
mechanisms to minimize the risk of misuse. This Policy will be implemented in accordance
with the schedule set forth in Table 1. On the date of full implementation outlined in Table 1,
this Policy supersedes the 2024 Framework for Nucleic Acid Synthesis Screening, as well as
any conflicting or inconsistent nucleic acid synthesis screening policy. Until that date, parties
should continue to draw from the relevant portions of the 2023 Health and Human Services
(HHS) Screening Framework Guidance for Providers and Users of Synthetic Nucleic Acids.



Scope and Covered Entities
To ensure that federally funded life sciences research benefits American citizens without
jeopardizing the Nation’s security, strength, and prosperity, this Policy sets forth the U.S.
Government-wide policy for screening orders of synthetic nucleic acids, with the objective of
applying to any order of 50 or more nucleotides in length, consistent with the baseline
recommended in the 2023 HHS Screening Framework Guidance for Providers and Users of
Synthetic Nucleic Acids.
This Policy applies to all recipients and subrecipients of Federal funds (i.e., Customers) that
procure synthetic nucleic acids or Benchtop Nucleic Acid Synthesis Equipment, but should be
implemented in a streamlined and scalable way that encourages its applicability to all synthetic
nucleic acid orders. As a condition of receiving Federal research funding, such entities will
procure synthetic nucleic acids and Benchtop Equipment only from Adherent Suppliers. This
policy supplements and does not supersede applicable law, including export controls and the
regulations governing Biological Select Agents and Toxins and the U.S. Government Policy for
Stopping High-Risk Life Sciences Research (July 20, 2026), which continue to apply.
A Provider, Manufacturer, or Third-Party Vendor that supplies synthetic nucleic acids or
Benchtop Equipment to Customers are either individually or jointly adherent to this Policy
when it (1) subjects every order to a Screener verified by the National Institute of Standards
and Technology (NIST), (2) conducts know-your-customer (KYC) verification, (3) reports
Flagged Orders to the Appropriate Federal Authorities (e.g., the Federal Bureau of
Investigation), (4) retains screening records and Customer interactions for a period of no-
less-than 8 years, and (5) on lawful request provides such records to NIST or Appropriate
Federal Authorities.

A verified Screener is a tool or service, or multiple thereof, that (1) assesses an order for
sequences of concern (SOC) and for customer legitimacy through order-level KYC
verification for SOCs, (2) issues a Screening Determination to a Supplier, and (3) reports
Flagged Orders to Appropriate Federal Authorities, as applicable. Screening tool verification
ensures that a Screener meets the screening standards issued by NIST as defined in this Policy
and is eligible to be used by a Supplier.



Screening System Structure
To adhere to this Policy, a Supplier submits every in-scope synthetic nucleic acid order
consisting of sequences 50 or more nucleotides in length to (or through) a verified Screener
before that order is fulfilled, and retains exclusive responsibility for the following:
   1. assesses each order, exercising judgement over the commercial relationship and the
      disposition of each;
   2. conducts onboarding KYC verification when it establishes a commercial relationship
      with a customer as well as periodic re-verification;
   3. retains all screening records and Customer interactions for a period of no less than 8
      years as outlined in this Policy; and
   4. on lawful request, provides records to NIST and Appropriate Federal Authorities as
      outlined in this Policy.
As the single control point for sequence risk screening, a verified Screener assesses all orders
of 50 nucleotides or more in length for SOCs and customer legitimacy (if the order contains a
SOC). This independent, order-level KYC screening ensures that for orders containing a SOC,
customer legitimacy is verified at two distinct points: by the Supplier at the establishment of
the commercial relationship, and by the verified Screener at the individual order level.
Reporting responsibility is shared by the verified Screener and the Supplier. The verified
Screener assesses each order, provides the Supplier with the Screening Determination and
reports any Flagged Orders to Appropriate Federal Authorities, as applicable. The Supplier
documents the Screening Determination, exercises independent judgement over the
disposition of the order, and reports to Appropriate Federal Authorities if screening concerns
remain unresolved.
Synthesis performed on Benchtop Equipment presents the same risk and this Policy therefore
requires that the control point be enforced at the equipment-level. Therefore, an adherent
Manufacturer or the Third-Party Vendor provides Benchtop Equipment which initiates
synthesis only when a valid authorization is issued by a verified Screener or for bounded offline
operation as verified in accordance with published NIST standards.

Screening of Orders Fulfilled by Suppliers
The final disposition of every order rests with the Supplier. A Supplier can flag an order or may
fulfill an order for which the screening determination records neither a SOC nor an unresolved
question of customer legitimacy.1 For Flagged Orders, the Supplier determines whether to
fulfill or deny, and that determination is not met by the use of a verified Screener. Records are
retained, and Flagged Orders reported with final disposition (fulfilled or denied), consistent
with the standard developed by NIST. The technical means by which orders are transmitted to
and assessed by a verified Screener, and by which a Screening Determination is issued, are
established by NIST as defined in this Policy.


Screening of Synthesis Performed on Benchtop Equipment
Benchtop Equipment operates outside the physical and procedural controls of a centralized
synthesis facility. Screening is therefore enforced by the equipment itself, which initiates
synthesis only on receipt of a valid, unexpired authorization from a verified Screener
corresponding to the submitted synthesis request, or during bounded offline operation
verified in accordance with published NIST standards. Absent such an authorization, the
equipment does not initiate synthesis. Manufacturers of obsolete Benchtop Equipment are
encouraged to buy back or offer trade-in opportunities towards machines that meet the
technical standards called for in this policy. NIST will establish the technical standards giving
effect to this section, including the form, issuance, cryptographic verification, physical
security, and validity of the authorization by which a verified Screener permits synthesis. These
standards shall include the two tiers of equipment behavior set forth below.
Tier 1: Networked Authorization
A unit of Benchtop Equipment in communication with a verified Screener initiates synthesis
only when a valid authorization is issued by that Screener for the request presented, and
declines any request that is unauthorized, expired, or inconsistent with the authorization
presented. Tier 1 is the default condition of operation for all Benchtop Equipment.
Tier 2: Bounded offline operation
A unit of Benchtop Equipment that cannot communicate with a verified Screener does not
initiate synthesis, except to the limited extent in which NIST verification can be used to
assure bounded offline operation as adherent to published standards. Where NIST verifies
such operation, it will specify the conditions and technical means by which synthesis may
be processed offline, the bounded operating limits that apply, and the records the
equipment shall retain and later reconcile with the Manufacturer or a verified Screener on
restoration of connectivity. Absent regular and necessary screening software updates, a
unit of Benchtop Equipment does not initiate synthesis while unable to communicate with
a verified Screener or unable to utilize the most updated screening software.

Roles and Responsibilities
NIST
Subject to the availability of funding, NIST, in collaboration with the HHS and the U.S.
Department of Agriculture (USDA), in consultation with the heads of other executive branch

   a) develop and publish the application procedures and implementation plan for Screener

Reporting to Appropriate Federal Authorities
A verified Screener will report each Flagged Order, Screening Determination, and reason for
Screening Determination to Appropriate Federal Authorities after issuing the Screening
Determination. The Supplier will, to the maximum extent allowed by law, report the Flagged
Orders to Appropriate Federal Authorities when Screening concerns remain unresolved
following review of the Screening Determination.
Appropriate Federal Authorities will act on reports of Flagged Orders and other suspicious
activity in accordance with their respective statutory authorities and missions. Appropriate
Federal Authorities, including the Federal Bureau of Investigation, will designate and maintain
a point of contact to receive such reports, and will work with NIST to incorporate standardized
reporting procedures and formats into the Screening standards issued under Rules and
Responsibilities section of this Policy.



Enforcement
Compliance with this Policy and with the guidance and standards issued under it is material to
the Government’s payment decisions for purposes of 31 U.S.C. 3729(b)(4). A recipient of
financial assistance, employer, or institution found to be in violation may be subject to
immediate revocation of ongoing Federal funding, to a period of ineligibility of up to 5 years for
Federal funds, and to such additional penalties as are consistent with existing Federal law and
regulation.

Consistent with subsection 4(b) of EO 14292, this Policy shall be reviewed and revised at least

a) “Adherent” means, with respect to a Supplier, that the Supplier:
   1. submits every order to a verified Screener for Screening;
   2. conducts onboarding KYC of the customer at the establishment of the commercial
      relationship, as well as periodic re-investigation;

   3. reports Flagged Orders to Appropriate Federal Authorities as directed in this Policy;
   4. retains records as required under Rules and Responsibilities of this Policy; and
   5. on lawful request, provides records to NIST and Appropriate Federal Authorities as
      required under Rules and Responsibilities of this Policy.
The terms “Adhere” and “Adherence” have corresponding meanings.
b) “Appropriate Federal Authorities” may include the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI),
   the Department of Commerce (DOC), and the Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS), each
   acting within its respective statutory authority to receive and act on reports of Flagged
   Orders and other suspicious activity.
c) “Benchtop Nucleic Acid Synthesis Equipment” means Benchtop Equipment sold by
   Manufacturers that is intended to be used to synthesize nucleic acids for use within a
   research laboratory or institution. Nucleic acid synthesis equipment not small enough to
   be placed on a benchtop (e.g., sits on the laboratory floor) is considered Benchtop Nucleic
   Acid Synthesis Equipment if it is sold for use by Customers.
d) “Covered Nation” means a nation defined in 10 USC 4872(f)(2).
e) “Customer” means a recipient or subrecipient of Federal funds that requests synthetic
   nucleic acids, Benchtop Equipment, or related access from a Supplier.
f) “Flagged Orders” means an order or synthesis request that a Screening Determination has
   identified as containing a SOC and unresolved concerns regarding customer legitimacy
   (KYC). All Flagged Orders must meet NIST reporting standards.
g) “Know Your Customer (KYC)” means the verification of legitimate use by a customer,
   comprising verification of the Customer’s identity, verification of the Customer’s
   institutional affiliation and authorization, and verification that the Customer’s stated use is
   consistent with the activities of the purchasing organization. KYC is performed at two
   points: by the Supplier at the establishment of the commercial relationship (“onboarding
   KYC”), and by the verified Screener at the level of the individual order (“order-level KYC”).
h) “Manufacturer” means an entity that produces Benchtop Equipment and distributes or
   sells it to Customers.
i) “Provider” means an entity that synthesizes synthetic nucleic acids and sells or otherwise
   furnishes them to Customers.
j) “Screener” means a tool or service, or a series of tools or services, that assesses an order
   for sequence risk and for customer legitimacy through KYC verification, issues a Screening
   Determination to a Supplier, and reports Flagged Orders to Appropriate Federal
   Authorities as described in Reporting to Appropriate Federal Authorities section of this
   Policy, as applicable. A Screener is a verified Screener when NIST has verified it against the
   standards issued under Rules and Responsibilities section of this Policy. Any Screener may
   be verified, whether operated by a Supplier for its own orders or offered by a third party as
   a service to any Supplier. Screeners should not develop tools, reside in, be under the
   control of, or send data to or through a Covered Nation.
k) “Screening” means the assessment of an order, by a verified Screener, for sequence risk
   and the verification of customer legitimacy through KYC verification, in conformity with
   the standards issued under Rules and Responsibilities section of this Policy.

l) “Screening Determination” means the signed result issued by a verified Screener on
   assessment of an order, recording whether the order presents a SOC, unresolved concerns
   regarding customer legitimacy (KYC), both, or neither, or whether the assessment is
   indeterminate for lack of sufficient technical evidence. A Screening Determination does

m) “Sequence of Concern (SOC)” means a nucleotide sequence 50 pairs or more in length

n) “Supplier” means a Provider, a Manufacturer, or a Third-Party Vendor, including core
   facilities. Suppliers are collectively subject to the obligations set forth in Rules and
   Responsibilities section of this Policy. Suppliers should not reside in or be under control of
   a Covered Nation.
o) “Third-Party Vendor” means an entity that distributes, resells, or otherwise furnishes to
   Customers synthetic nucleic acids or Benchtop Equipment produced by another entity,
   including but not limited to regional distributors and resellers of both new and used
