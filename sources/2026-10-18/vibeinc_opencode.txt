OpenCode confirms a breach and tells affected Console users that AI provider keys saved in their workspaces may have been exposed
Incident &middot; OpenCode Console / cloud workspaces (saved third-party AI provider keys) &middot; HIGH
Incident date Night of Oct 16–17, 2026, per OpenCode ("last night" in its Oct 17 post).
Disclosed Oct 17, 2026 (email to affected users that morning; public statement on X at 1:56 p.m. ET).
Added here Oct 17, 3:05 PM ET
Last verified Oct 17, 2026
Summary
OpenCode, the open-source AI coding agent made by Anomaly, said on X on Oct 17, 2026 that it "had a security breach last night related to a large scale distillation attack that impacted 1.4% of OpenCode Console users", and that it had notified all of them by email that morning. It added that users who did not get an email were not affected, and that those affected were a subset of users with external providers. The email, which recipients shared publicly under the subject "Action Required: Potential provider key breach", says OpenCode identified unauthorized access to a legacy system it was in the process of decommissioning, and that third-party provider keys saved in a user's workspace (it names OpenAI and Anthropic as examples) may have been exposed. It asks users to revoke and replace those keys at each provider, update any apps that use them and review recent usage. Some users reported unexpected spend on provider keys they say were stored only in their OpenCode account. Those reports are self-reported and we have not verified them. OpenCode said in its first post that the incident is ongoing. Later on Oct 17, OpenCode's Dax Raad said the breach involves the hosted OpenCode Console, not the OpenCode app itself, and Anthropic said it had revoked Claude API keys it believes were affected. OpenCode has not said how the attacker got in, how many keys were involved, or how the breach relates to what it calls a distillation attack.
Severity
HIGH High (VibeIncidents editorial rating). OpenCode confirms a breach, and its email tells affected users that third-party AI provider keys saved in their workspaces may have been exposed. A leaked provider key can be used to run up charges on its owner's account, and some users report that happened. OpenCode puts the affected group at 1.4% of Console users and says it emailed all of them. It has not said how many keys were involved, and the incident is still being investigated, so this rating may change.

How it happened
Most of what is known comes from OpenCode's email, as recipients shared it, and its post on X. The email says OpenCode identified unauthorized access to a legacy system that it was in the process of decommissioning, and that third-party provider keys saved in a user's workspace may have been exposed. OpenCode itself runs on your machine and keeps provider keys there. OpenCode's Dax Raad said on Oct 17 that the breach involves the OpenCode Console, its hosted service, "not OpenCode itself", and that keys used in the app "never come to us". The exposed keys are the ones users chose to save in their Console workspace. On X, OpenCode linked the breach to "a large scale distillation attack" but has not explained how that connects to the access to the legacy system. It also has not said how the access happened, when it was detected, how many keys were involved, whether the keys were encrypted at rest, or whether anything besides provider keys was in that system. We won't guess at those details, and we will update this entry if OpenCode publishes them.
How to check your app
If you ever saved an OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, OpenRouter or other provider key in an OpenCode workspace, revoke it in that provider's dashboard and create a new one, even if you see no unusual activity and got no email.
Anthropic says it has revoked Claude API keys it believes were affected, so an Anthropic key you saved in OpenCode Console may already have stopped working. Create a new one and update anything that used the old key.
OpenCode says users who did not get its email were not affected. Check the inbox tied to your OpenCode account, including spam, for "Action Required: Potential provider key breach".
Review usage per key at each provider (OpenAI, Anthropic Console, Google Cloud or AI Studio, OpenRouter and others) for the past several days, especially overnight Oct 16–17, 2026, and note anything you didn't run. Most providers show usage by key or project.
If you see charges you didn't make, contact the provider's billing support with the key ID, the dates and the fact that OpenCode notified you of a possible exposure.
Set monthly spend limits or budget alerts at each provider, turn off auto-reload where you don't need it, and use a separate key per tool so one leak can be revoked without breaking everything else.
Update any apps, CI jobs or scripts that used the old key so they don't fail silently after you revoke it.
