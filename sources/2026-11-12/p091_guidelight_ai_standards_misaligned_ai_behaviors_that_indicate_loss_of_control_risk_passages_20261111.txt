Divergent priorities Attribution November 11, 2026 Misaligned AI behaviors that indicate loss-of-control risk Ten kinds of AI misbehavior that developers should monitor, investigate, and report. See this article on our Substack blog A fundamental driver of loss-of-control risk is misalignment, wherein an AI system does not reliably try to behave as its developers intend. So far, warning signs of misalignment have often been observable in what AI systems think and do. This post lays out ten kinds of AI misbehavior that are warning signs of misalignment and loss-of-control risk. Our goal is to give developers, researchers, and policymakers a shared list of what to look for while these signals remain observable. Frontier AI developers should monitor, investigate, and report these behaviors. This would advance scientific understanding of misalignment and help reduce the chance of large-scale accidents. PDF version (opens in a new tab) Formatted-for-AI .md What developers should do We recommend a three-part procedure for frontier AI developers, building on Guidelight’s standards : Monitor these categories internally. To form an accurate picture of misalignment in their AI systems, developers should scan their AI systems’ thinking and actions for each category below, and track how often each occurs. This requires robust monitoring systems that cover the overwhelming majority of internal AI activity. Investigate what they find. Developers don’t yet know why AI systems become misaligned or how to reliably correct it. Each instance of misbehavior represents evidence on those fundamental questions. Root-cause investigation of the most severe instances can help identify what caused the misbehavior and inform how to address it. Report what they observe. Without reporting , the public has no visibility into these behaviors. Reports of notable instances and their causes tell other developers what to look for, let external researchers study the evidence independently, and help policymakers understand what is happening and how to respond. 1 1 As of publication, we consider OpenAI’s misalignment reporting framework the approach that comes closest to this, though no company has fully implemented Guidelight’s standard for incident reporting . Even if they do not observe any of the behaviors below, developers cannot assume that their AI systems are aligned. Misalignment has often been visible in an AI’s reasoning, because models have been designed to “think out loud” while operating. However, this visibility is imperfect, and emerging evidence suggests that it is becoming harder to detect misalignment from an AI’s reasoning. 2 2 An AI’s chain-of-thought reasoning can reveal misalignment, but it provides an incomplete view of the model’s underlying cognition. Preserving this visibility requires careful decision-making in how models are developed. See Korbak et al., “ Chain of Thought Monitorability: A New and Fragile Opportunity for AI Safety ” (2025). Monitorability refers to the ability to detect an AI’s misaligned behavior, including from evidence in its reasoning. When OpenAI announced GPT-6 Astra, it reported that the system’s “monitorability has decreased relative to GPT-5.6 Sol.” See sections 1 and 9.1 of the GPT-6 Astra System Card . Therefore, even if an AI’s reasoning looks benign, that alone is not sufficient evidence that it is trying to act as intended. Our Alignment standard contains more detail about how to probe for possible misalignment, though it remains an imperfect science. How to read this list The categories below are signals of misalignment. Observing these patterns of behavior indicates that an AI system might be pursuing goals its developer didn’t intend, in ways that could contribute to loss-of-control risk. While these behaviors are typically cause for concern, in some contexts they could be consistent with what a developer intends. The scope of every category includes thinking about how to carry out the behavior, attempting it, and succeeding at it. The list is neither comprehensive nor ranked in order of importance, and the boundaries between categories are imprecise. In future work, we plan to share more about how developers ought to prioritize among these categories. We welcome feedback on this list at feedback@guidelight.ai or on Substack .
