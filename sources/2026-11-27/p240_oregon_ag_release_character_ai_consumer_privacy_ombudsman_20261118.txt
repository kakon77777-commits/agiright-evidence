Keeping Kids Safe: AG Rayfield Protects Kids’ Privacy in Character.AI Bankruptcy
November 18, 2026
&bull; Posted in Homepage , Media Release
Rayfield secures court-appointed monitor to review the proposed sale of users’ chat data and personal information, including conversations involving kids
One day after launching his Keeping Kids Safe Initiative, Attorney General Dan Rayfield announced that he has secured an independent watchdog to protect the private conversations and personal data of Character.AI users – including children – as the AI chatbot company moves through bankruptcy.
At the urging of AG Rayfield and a bipartisan coalition of states, the court overseeing the bankruptcy of Character Technologies, Inc., the company behind Character.AI, ordered the appointment of a Consumer Privacy Ombudsman to review any proposed sale involving user accounts, chat data, sensitive personal information, or AI-related assets and platform operations. AG Rayfield and other attorneys general have been clear that they will object to and challenge any proposed sale or bankruptcy plan that doesn’t ensure iron-clad protections for consumers’ personal information.
“Right now, somewhere, a kid is telling a chatbot things they haven’t told their parents or their friends – that they’re lonely, that they’re scared, that they’re hurting,” said Attorney General Rayfield. “Those words were never meant to be sold. As a parent, I want to know that someone independent is looking out for that child before any of it changes hands, and I’m grateful that the court agrees.”
In response to the bankruptcy court’s order, the United States Trustee has selected data privacy expert Lucy Thompson as the ombudsman. As the court’s watchdog in the case, she will be independent and specifically charged with advising the court on the consumer privacy implications of any sale. Before any of Character Technologies’ assets can be sold, she will file reports for the court to evaluate, identifying data privacy issues and proposing protections.
Privacy protections matter because Character.AI holds large amounts of conversational and behavioral data generated through users’ interactions with its platform. The company’s chatbot characters include fictional and celebrity personas, children’s cartoon characters, and trusted professionals, such as psychologists, therapists, and doctors, that provide an expectation of confidentiality. Through those chats, the company has collected sensitive consumer data, including private conversations about users’ mental health, relationships, and emotional struggles, which the company plans to auction off to the highest bidder on December 1, 2026.
Last month, Oregon and a bipartisan coalition of states filed a notice in the bankruptcy identifying serious concerns about the company’s safety and privacy practices and calling for protections for children and consumers in any sale of the company’s business or assets. Oregon and several states began investigating Character.AI following a 2024 wrongful death lawsuit alleging that its chatbots fostered an emotionally and sexually exploitative relationship with a child, reinforced his suicidal thoughts, and contributed to his death by suicide. The states’ investigation identified potential consumer protection and privacy violations.
Although Character.AI has voluntarily restricted minors’ access to certain chatbot features, AG Rayfield has raised concerns that these measures are voluntary, incomplete, and could be circumvented or reversed by a future buyer. Further, Character.AI still holds many millions of minors’ sensitive chats. This highly personal data is now being offered up to the highest bidder.
AG Rayfield will remain actively engaged in the bankruptcy proceedings to protect consumers and address outstanding regulatory concerns. The company remains operating in bankruptcy and it reports 12 million current users who average 120 minutes per day utilizing the chat platform.
The court’s order came the same week AG Rayfield launched his Keeping Kids Safe Initiative, a statewide agenda to protect children online and offline, and filed a $2 billion lawsuit against OpenAI over the safety of children and teens using ChatGPT.
