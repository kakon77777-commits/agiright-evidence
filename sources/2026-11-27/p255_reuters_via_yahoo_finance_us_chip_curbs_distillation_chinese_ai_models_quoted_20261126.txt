Z.AI, also known as Zhipu AI, had planned to release its next flagship model, GLM-5.5, this month but has yet to do so, two people familiar with the matter said on condition of anonymity.

The model, designed with roughly 3 trillion parameters - a measure of its size - has been held up partly by increasingly effective safeguards against distillation, one of the people said.

Z.AI had hoped to complete training and release the model by year-end, but the new US computing restrictions could push its launch into the first quarter of 2027 or later, the person said.

The AI developer internally expects its usual six-month interval between major releases could stretch to 12 months or longer if its overseas computing access is cut off entirely, the person said, adding that computing capacity available in China could not make up the shortfall.

The previously unreported details show how efforts by US AI companies OpenAI and Anthropic to prevent distillation, a technique in which developers train models using answers generated by more capable ones, have slowed Chinese AI development.
