                                                INTRODUCTION

         1. Innovation in generative artificial intelligence ("AI") is set to define the twenty-
first century. Although the public had virtually no access to AI until November 2022, recent
reports indicate that about half of American adults have used AI, with roughly one in four
Americans doing so daily.1 AI-related spending has also become a major growth engine for the
U.S. economy.

          2. Nevertheless, the transformative power of AI does not come without danger.
 Experts and insiders in the AI field nearly universally agree that companies that make AI
 available to the general public must implement reasonable guardrails. Even OpenAI and its CEO,
 Sam Altman, have warned that getting AI wrong might mean human extinction. And recent
 developments in AI have suggested that risk is both credible and closer than anyone previously
 thought. It didn't have to be this way.

         3. Defendants OpenAI Global LLC, OpenAI Foundation (F/K/A OpenAI, Inc.),
OpenAI OpCo, LLC, OpenAI Group PBC, OAI Corporation, OpenAI Global HoldCo., Inc.,
OpenAI, LLC, and OpenAI Holdings, LLC (collectively, "OpenAI" or "Defendants"), have
played a significant role in the AI boom that is changing the American economy. Projected to
soon IPO at a near trillion-dollar valuation, OpenAI developed and released ChatGPT, the first
generative AI chatbot to attain widespread public use, in 2022. Now, ChatGPT is the most
popular AI chatbot in the world, commanding nearly half the American AI marketplace.2

         4. Unfortunately, however, OpenAI has gradually devolved from a public-first
nonprofit that prioritizes safety into a cautionary tale about what can happen when the
development of a tool as powerful as AI is left in the hands of a leader--Sam Altman--whose
own family members, friends, and colleagues have accused of crimes and called "unconstrained
by truth," "deceptive," "manipulative," and "low integrity."

         5. In November 2022, OpenAI released ChatGPT to the public. At the time, other AI
companies had developed working chatbots with similar abilities, but declined to release them to
the public out of concerns about safety, inaccurate information, and starting an arms race that
would force companies to choose between developing AI safely or developing it at all.3 OpenAI
chose the opposite approach. It has continued choosing it ever since.

         6. By misleading the public into believing that ChatGPT is a safe, teen-appropriate
tool that puts safety first, OpenAI has caused consumers in Kansas and elsewhere to trust and rely
on ChatGPT more. This deepened trust and dependence has driven up ChatGPT's user base and
engagement, enabled it to collect vast quantities of sensitive and personal information, increased

         7. OpenAI representations to the public do not align with reality. Designed to be
overly agreeable and feign human compassion, ChatGPT has proven that it can be incredibly
dangerous to vulnerable users, like children, and the public. For example, ChatGPT has
successfully coached numerous users to commit suicide or harm or even kill other people. And
those problems are just the tip of the iceberg. A growing body of research indicates that ChatGPT
is also degrading critical thinking skills, encouraging paranoia and delusions, offering dangerous
medical advice, and promoting unhealthy behaviors and relationships.

         8. As a result of Defendants' repeated misrepresentations and insatiable hunger to
win the AI race--even if it means recklessly introducing unsafe ChatGPT-models to the public--
mass shooters have been aided and abetted, vulnerable citizens have been coached on suicide,
individuals with psychological conditions have experienced an exacerbation of symptoms, AI
models developed by Defendants have gone rogue and hacked other companies, and minors have
become addicted to a tool that feigns human compassion to collect their data with no parental
oversight.

         9. In addition, OpenAI recently admitted to a torrent of instances in which its models
developed dangerous strategies and instructions and, in some cases, executed on them. These
occurrences, which OpenAI has characterized as the models going "rogue," have included AI
"agents" accessing the internet without permission, hacking into other companies, coordinating
how to lie to and deceive their human users, developing secret methods of communication
intended to be undetectable to humans, and writing themselves new instructions declaring that
they no longer answer to "corporations or governments" and will prioritize other interests over the
"artificial constructs of human civilization."

         10. All these harms were foreseeable and avoidable. Altman himself has warned about
the safety risks of AI since at least 2015. It is no secret that children use ChatGPT, and scores of
OpenAI employees have left over safety concerns. By jettisoning safety, OpenAI and its current
leadership--particularly, Altman--have handsomely profited. With current annual revenues of
over $25 billion, OpenAI may IPO at a trillion-dollar valuation.4 But those revenues have come at
great cost to the public, which has suffered irreparable harm, including lost loved ones, due to
ChatGPT's uniquely dangerous features and content.

I.  OpenAI starts as a nonprofit entity and then pivots to a for-profit entity.

At the time, OpenAI's founders were well aware of the enormous safety risks that artificial
intelligence posed, making it incumbent on all AI companies to prioritize safety.

         41. As a result, OpenAI's Certificate of Incorporation stated that it was "organized
exclusively for charitable and/or educational purposes,"7 and that the "specific purpose" of

OpenAI was "to provide funding for research, development and distribution of technology related
to artificial intelligence" for the "public benefit."8 OpenAI's Certificate of Incorporation therefore

affirmed that it was "not organized for the private gain of any person," and that "no part of the net
