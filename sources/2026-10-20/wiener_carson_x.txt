Post 1: A lot of discussion about Anthropic's proposal for an AI framework. Let me start by saying, it's hard to put pen to paper and offer a detailed vision for a comprehensive framework. Credit to Anthropic for their serious effort. That said, parts of the draft raise real concerns. | Unfortunately the bill text that’s circulating does not meet the minimum requirements necessary for federal guardrails to keep AI development safe. | Critically, the proposed bill allows developers to set safety standards and loosen those same standards over time, leaving AI companies to write their own rules. This represents a status quo that we know doesn't work. The responsibility for crafting binding federal safety standards must rest with the government. Those standards should be strengthened as AI systems become more capable. | The bill also fails to provide the government a role in testing or inspection authority, instead depending on private markets that are not yet mature to hold AI companies accountable. Under the bill, evaluators self-certify, registration carries no power to deny or revoke, and the bill bars any agency standard from being “a condition of engagement.” | It's also worth raising issues with the bill’s preemption provisions. The bill would preempt existing protections in multiple states. There’s also no sunset on preemption and no condition tying preemption to the strength of federal guardrails. Under our own proposal, preemption is used only as a floor, not a ceiling for AI safeguards. 1/4
