GPT-6 as a Universal Foundation Model for Protein Biology: Beyond Specialized Models
Masatoshi Uehara, Edward Zhou, Joshua Park, Morgan Griffiths, Austin Clyde, Suyash Shringarpure, Yuan Yuan, Kevin Rao (Life Science Team, OpenAI); Anton Bushuiev, Michael Yu (contractors, OpenAI); Kathryn M. Collins, Jesse Hendricks, Puwanat Sangkhapreecha, Lee-Shin Chu, Sharrol Bachas (Onava AI); Joy Jiao
November 24, 2026

Abstract (first sentences): Specialized models have transformed protein structure prediction and design, yet incorporating new molecular constraints often requires modifying the software or retraining the model. Here we show that general-purpose language models in the GPT-6 family can perform diverse protein modeling and design tasks using only reasoning and code, with natural language as a common means of specifying molecular constraints.

Footnote on the first page: The OpenAI Life Science Team leads this project. Onava contributed by conducting a protein design campaign in the wet lab.

Section 7, Conclusion: The GPT-6 model series demonstrates strong intrinsic capabilities in protein biology, with a notable exception in end-to-end structure prediction. Our protein design campaigns still rely on specialized structure prediction models, particularly for filtering candidates.

Section 8.1, Models and Evaluation Settings: We evaluated GPT-5.6, GPT-6, GPT-6.2 and internal models. In the standard setting for Sections 2-4, GPT could write and run code but could not call pretrained protein models or browse the internet. The experimental campaigns used a pre-release version of GPT-6.2.
