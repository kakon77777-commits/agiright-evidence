AG Blanche, FBI’s Patel dodge questions about Jensen Huang investigation

Attorney General Todd Blanche and FBI Director Kash Patel on Tuesday dodged questions about the conclusions of an investigation President Donald Trump cited to push Nvidia's board to bring back Jensen Huang as CEO.

Asked by a reporter whether the Department of Justice had investigated Huang and the results of that probe, Blanche said, "What the president said is that he talked to a lot of people going into the decision that he made to encourage the board to reinstate him."

"And so I'm not going to get into communications I have with the president," Blanche said at a news conference in Washington.

In an interview with CNBC on Monday, Trump was asked what led him to personally confirm to Nvidia's board that Huang was not a national security risk.

"We've done a very thorough investigation, and the investigation showed that he did, I mean, he had bad judgment having to do with somewhat unrelated incidents, but he did nothing wrong," Trump replied.

Trump in a Truth Social post on Monday said the U.S. government had no reason to believe Huang's return to the company "poses any risk to National Security," and that Blanche had confirmed the government "does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct."

Blanche on Tuesday said, "Nvidia is a good corporate citizen."

"Obviously, if Nvidia was the target, or was the subject of some criminal investigation, or they were obstructing or thwarting our investigation, then that wouldn't be the message that the president is saying to the American people," he said.

The DOJ and Commerce Department on Nov. 17 said they were investigating Megaspeed for potential diversion of Nvidia chips in violation of U.S. export law.

Asked how the DOJ could rule out Jensen Huang having potential criminal liability given the fact that the investigation remains ongoing, Blanche replied, "So, when you say the export investigation is still ongoing, I don't know what you're talking about."

"We have hundreds, if not more, export investigations going at any time at the Department of Justice," Blanche said.
