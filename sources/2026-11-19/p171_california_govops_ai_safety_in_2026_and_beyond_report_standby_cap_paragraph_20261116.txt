More critically, the legislature should provide the state with standby authority to cap automated AI R&D when the level of risk is unacceptable. For example, the state should be able to cap the share of compute a developer can devote to automation of AI R&D. As broader measurements of AI-led R&D become more mature, the state should also use those metrics. We do not recommend that the
21

state impose a cap immediately. But the state must possess this authority if it finds that a developer’s automated AI R&D has exceeded its committed thresholds or is advancing faster than its safeguards and evaluations can keep up, creating a material risk of catastrophic harm. The cap should be limited in time, reviewed periodically, and lifted when the issue has been addressed and an IVO verifies that adequate safeguards are in place.
The state would set the cap level and measurement methods, informed by work with IVOs.
