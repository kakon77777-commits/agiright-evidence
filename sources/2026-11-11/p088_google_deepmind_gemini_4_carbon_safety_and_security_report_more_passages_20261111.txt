our FSF, we conduct a critical capability assessment prior to the first external deployment of a new frontier model. For external deployment of subsequent versions of the model, we determine whether a substantial modification has been made and whether a further critical capability assessment is required if (1) the model has meaningful new capabilities or material increases in performance; and (2) we believe such a material capability increase could undermine the justification for why the risks stemming from the model are acceptable. To understand if a subsequent version of the model has meaningful new capabilities or material increases in performance relative to the most recent model version subject to a critical capability assessment, we conduct a Material Capability Change (MCC) assessment. We do this by measuring its general capability using GCI v2, our internal General Capability Index modeled on the Epoch Capabilities Index (ECI).1 Like the ECI, GCI v2 combines a model’s performance across many diverse benchmarks into a single overall capability score. We treat a difference in GCI scores as material if it exceeds four months of capability progress, estimated from a line of best fit over frontier models. Across historical frontier Gemini releases, this general capability score has increased at a rate of roughly 4.7 GCI points per month. 1 GCI v2 updates the methodology and score scaling of GCI v1 (reported in Section 3.4.2.2 of the Gemini 4 Argon Safety and Security Report).
