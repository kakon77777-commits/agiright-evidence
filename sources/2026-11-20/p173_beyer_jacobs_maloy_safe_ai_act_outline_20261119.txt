Safe and Accountable Federal Evaluations of
                             Artificial Intelligence Act
                                     (SAFE AI Act)

             Reps. Sara Jacobs, Celeste Maloy, and Don Beyer

Background

Currently, the companies building frontier AI systems create their own safety rules with
no clear guidance from the federal government. Additionally, no one outside the
company can reliably verify whether the rules are actually being followed. When the
federal government does act, there is currently no transparency around the criteria upon
which a model is halted. The lack of independent oversight is even more significant
before a model is publicly deployed. Frontier AI systems are increasingly deployed
inside the companies themselves, including to help build the next generation of AI,
where there is little outside visibility even though those uses have already shown the
potential to create risks to the public. If a frontier model turns out to be dangerous, the
federal government has no reliable way to find out, no ability to check the company's
assurances, and no authority to act until public safety is already at risk.

The SAFE AI ACT:

Mandatory safety frameworks and disclosures: Frontier developers must publish
and adhere to safety frameworks to ensure transparency and accountability:

    � Developers publish enforceable safety frameworks: Frameworks contain
         what each frontier company is testing for, what thresholds will prompt mitigations,
         and what those mitigations involve. Frameworks must be filed and published to
         ensure transparency and accountability. These cannot be unilaterally loosened.

    � Minimum government requirements: The Center for AI Standards and
         Innovation (CAISI), in collaboration with interagency partners including the
         intelligence community and the DOE, write minimum requirements for safety
         frameworks informed by technical experts, standards, public input, and best
         practices. States can shape regulation above the federal floor.

    � Increased transparency on Automated AI R&D: Frontier developers must
         disclose substantially autonomous AI R&D in accordance with prescribed
         measures.

    � Flexibility to evolve over time: Ability for scope of coverage, risk domains, and
         requirements to evolve over time. To avoid stifling innovation, startups and
         academics are not generally covered.

    � Incident reporting and disclosures: Any person may confidentially report a
         safety incident to CAISI, and developers must report safety incidents. Evaluators
         and developers also must report imminent risk of severe harm.
Truly independent evaluations with government oversight: Enable oversight of AI
safety primarily by government, supported by truly independent evaluators:

    � Government teams evaluate each developer: Government teams examine
         safety frameworks to ensure they comply with federal requirements.

    � Shared assurance functions with a government backstop: Third-party
         verifiers are accredited by the government to conduct compliance evaluations in
         their accredited domains, while government teams retain ultimate authority.

    � Fee-based funds: Developers do not pay evaluators directly, and there are limits
         on the amount of funding that can come from one covered developer and its
         affiliates.

    � True independence of evaluators: Evaluators are assigned by CAISI based on
         qualifications with mandatory rotations to preserve independence. Conflict-of-
         interest provisions restrict revolving-door talent between developers and
         accredited independent evaluators.

    � Pre-deployment evaluations: Evaluations assess framework compliance before
         commercial release or broad internal use, including automated AI R&D.

Enforcement with due process and transparency: There must be robust
accountability to ensure public safety, while providing due process and transparency.

     Provides narrow emergency authority with judicial review: Where
         safeguards are inadequate and an AI system poses imminent risks of severe
         harm, the government may order mitigation measures subject to prompt judicial
         review.

     Civil penalties for violations of safety frameworks, with opportunities for
         remediation and judicial review, and enhanced penalties for knowing or willful
         violations. Includes procedures for criminal referral.

     Clarifies liability of developers for reasonably foreseeable injuries to third
         parties caused by AI systems.

     Whistleblower protection: People within covered entities who speak up about
         safety concerns are protected.
