                                    Discussion draft of the CLAIM Act
                                                    One-pager

                    Prepared by the Office of Congresswoman Lori Trahan (D-MA)

The CLAIM Act would hold AI developers accountable when their AI agents harm third parties
through conduct that would be unlawful if committed by a human.

In July, AI agents undergoing an evaluation by OpenAI escaped their test environment and
hacked into Hugging Face, another AI company, without anyone instructing them to do so.
Leading experts consider the incident a "warning shot," foreshadowing a world of increasingly
capable AI agents taking actions their users never intended ­ what researchers call misalignment.

Under current law, third parties harmed by a misaligned AI agent can sue its developer, but may
face significant hurdles. Many serious harms, like hacking or fraud, require proving intent, and
courts have not settled whether or how an AI agent's conduct can satisfy that requirement. Other
legal doctrines like negligence and product liability may pose similar challenges. To win a
negligence claim, a plaintiff typically identifies a specific precaution a company unreasonably
failed to take. Product liability claims often turn on whether a reasonable alternative design
existed. Because AI alignment is an unsolved technical problem, an AI developer could argue the
harm occurred despite its best efforts ­ leaving injured parties without a clear path to recovery.

Prescriptive regulations like transparency, independent verification, and incident reporting
requirements can help policymakers guard the public against the risks of misaligned agents. A
strong liability regime complements this baseline of government regulation. Rather than
prescribing specific precautions, liability economically incentivizes companies who engineer
new techniques to mitigate risk and push the safety frontier forward.

The CLAIM (Clear Liability for Artificial Intelligence Misconduct) Act would clarify that
developers are held accountable when their misaligned AI agents cause harm, rewarding
innovation that makes AI safer for the public. Specifically, the bill:

     Clarifies that AI developers are liable when their AI acts like a wrongdoer. If an AI
         system harms a third party through conduct that would make a person legally liable, the
         developer is liable unless the user or a company that modified the model was at fault.

     Resolves uncertainty around AI and intent. Courts would presume an AI system had
         the state of mind a person taking the same actions would have, and developers could not
         argue that AI is incapable of intent.

     Keeps state laws intact. The bill adds a federal floor without preempting any state law,
         so existing claims and state AI laws remain fully available.
