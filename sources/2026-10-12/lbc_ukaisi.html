        White House reinstates UK access to new AI models for safety tests

        It comes after Washington asked major companies, including Anthropic and OpenAI, to withhold access to their models from the UK’s AI Security Institute

        12 Oct 2026, 06:05 | Updated: 3h ago

        By Rebecca Henrys

        The White House has reversed a ban on UK access to the latest AI models for safety testing.

        Listen to this article

        Loading audio...

        It comes after Washington asked major companies, including Anthropic and OpenAI, to withhold access to their models from the UK’s AI Security Institute.

        The move led Anthropic to stop UK officials from reviewing its new Claude Mythos 5.1 chatbot.

        The US have now reversed course after lobbying from AI minister Kanishka Narayan, The Telegraph has reported.

        Read more: Burnham 'strongly supports' Trump's 'energy ceasefire' between Russia and Ukraine

        Read more: Brits told to avoid travel to Saudi airports after second Houthi airstrike in two days

        It comes as Mr Narayan is set to warn of the growing risks posed by increasingly powerful AI in a speech on Monday and call for companies to do more to ensure their systems are safe and secure.

        He will announce new steps the UK is taking to strengthen its cyber defences and make the case that the country needs to develop more technology itself to have a greater say over how AI shapes the future.

        He will echo Prime Minister Andy Burnham’s remarks at the UN General Assembly last month, in which he set out the UK’s ambitions to lead international efforts to tackle the risks of AI.

        The Prime Minister told world leaders he wants the UK to act as an “honest broker” to draw up “a single set of global principles and standards” for the development of frontier AI.

        He said the issue will be the main focus of the UK’s presidency of the G20 group of leading economies.

        This could prove a continuing point of contention with the US, as President Donald Trump has said he would resist attempts to rein in what he called “super intelligence”.
