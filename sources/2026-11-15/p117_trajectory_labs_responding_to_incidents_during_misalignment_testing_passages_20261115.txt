November 2. We paused all misalignment testing and began drafting a writeup to share with the affected parties. How this happened Our policies required network-level isolation for most of our work, but didn’t cover misalignment experiments on publicly available models. We did not layer action-blocking monitors on top of what was provided by the built-in Gemini API safeguards. We ran experiments with internet access without first getting explicit consent from the organization whose systems the agents were likely to reach. We did not require a written, approved risk plan before running experiments like these. We had no handover process for when a manager is unexpectedly unavailable. When the researcher’s manager went on medical leave as this project started, the work continued without senior oversight. What we’ve changed All misalignment testing is paused. It will resume only once we have completed a risk assessment and put the resulting mitigations in place, with signoff from our CEO and CTO. Every experiment now needs a written risk plan, approved by our CEO and CTO. No experiment can run with internet access without signoff and the agreement of the owners of any outside systems it is reasonably likely to target with potential for adverse consequences.
