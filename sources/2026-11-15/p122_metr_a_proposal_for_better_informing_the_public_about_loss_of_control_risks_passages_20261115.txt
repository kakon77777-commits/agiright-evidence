AI R&D acceleration: Developers should work with external groups to share crucially missing evidence about acceleration in internal capabilities and in algorithmic progress. A concrete proposal to frontier developers for improving public evidence about these questions through risk assessment exercises, in which METR would write public reports based on interviews with approved staff and access to internal datasets and systems on company devices. Developers would be able to redact any information they deem sensitive, and choose what access they grant METR for their investigations. The key asks we make of developers for these assessments are: Transparency about the scope, access, redactions and other key limitations of the exercises. Approved staff may speak privately to METR about in-scope topics, to ensure they feel free to share potentially concerning evidence on those topics. Timely publication of METR-written reports within the agreed-upon scope, subject to a redaction process. Limitations of our proposal. Our proposal does not require true employee-level access, as operationalized in a recent public letter (point 5 in the letter), and there is currently no way to be fully protected from retaliation due to adverse findings (point 4). While developers would be able to redact any sensitive information and choose what access they grant METR, they would have to agree to our key terms: Giving a small set of METR staff access to relevant data on company devices for an agreed upon period (likely weeks or a few months), and support from a technical point of contact during the investigations. Giving METR the opportunity to interview approved staff privately, without a company representative present and without screening answers, to ensure they feel free to raise potential concerns. METR would be happy to limit total interview time to reduce staff time requirements. Giving METR in-principle editorial independence to write a report covering the agreed upon scope (subject to redactions), and allowing METR to comment on redactions, access limitations and terms.
