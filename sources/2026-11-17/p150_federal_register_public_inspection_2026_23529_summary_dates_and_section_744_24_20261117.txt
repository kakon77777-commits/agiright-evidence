Addressing Remote Access to Advanced Semiconductors
AGENCY: Bureau of Industry and Security, Department of Commerce.
ACTION: Interim final rule.
SUMMARY: In this interim final rule (IFR), the Department of Commerce is establishing new
rules to prevent certain foreign actors from leveraging advanced computing power based on U.S.
technology to threaten U.S. national security and foreign policy interests. First, this rule
establishes new restrictions which will apply for exports, reexports, or transfers (in-country) to
overseas data centers that remotely supply significant volumes of compute to companies
headquartered in Country Group D:5 or Macau. These restrictions will undermine the ability of
companies headquartered in countries of concern from using U.S. advanced Super Intelligence
(SI) semiconductors to train advanced SI models. Data centers, foreign foundries, and chip
exporters are required to perform robust due diligence on their customers, including any direct or
indirect ownership or control by persons in Country Group D:5 or Macau, as part of complying
with this new control. The license requirement will not apply to commercial recommender
systems, such as those used by online retailers or social media companies, as these functions do
not pose the same national security threat. Second, in addition to the current license requirements
for advanced semiconductors, a license will be required to export, reexport, or transfer (in-
country) advanced semiconductors to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, or Brazil (and
any additional country designated by the Secretary of Commerce, in consultation with the
Secretary of State or the Assistant to the President for National Security Affairs) – countries
which present a higher-than-normal risk of diversion. The license requirement for these five
countries will not apply to semiconductors destined to companies headquartered in, or whose
ultimate parent company is headquartered in, the United States, provided certain reporting
requirements are met. Commerce will be actively monitoring for countries that tolerate evasion
of this IFR and will consider a range of consequences for such countries, such as import
restrictions, new license requirements on advanced semiconductor exports, downgrading the
country’s status in the U.S. export control regime, and Entity List designations and temporary
export prohibitions for foreign persons involved in such activities. Third, this IFR amends the
presumptions which front-end chips fabricators and chip packagers face when exporting
advanced-node logic chips. These amendments reduce the number of pathways to overcome the
presumptions for chip designers located in destinations which face a license requirement for SI
chips. This change gives the U.S. government critical oversight over companies headquartered in
certain countries that seek to leverage advanced semiconductor production capabilities in third
countries. Finally, the IFR formally rescinds the regulations implementing the Biden-era AI
Diffusion Rule. The AI Diffusion Rule has never been enforced. The regulatory changes in this
rule formalize the rescission of the AI Diffusion Rule, which the Department of Commerce
Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) announced in May 2025.

§ 744.24 Restrictions on exports, reexports, and transfers (in-country)
of certain advanced computing items for use in data centers for end-uses and destinations of
concern. Section 744.24 provides, in addition to the license requirements set forth elsewhere in
the EAR, a prohibition on exports, reexports, or transfers (in-county) of advanced SI items
without a license if, at the time of export, reexport, or transfer (in-country), you have
“knowledge” that the item will be or is intended to be used in a data center end-use that is
prohibited under paragraphs (a)(1) of § 744.24.

         You may not knowingly export, reexport, or transfer (in-county) items specified in
paragraph (a) of § 744.24 for use in a data center to or within any destination worldwide that will
provide access to more than 12,000,000 “aggregate TPP” (either at one data center or through
‘linked data centers’ (including digitally-linked infrastructure, not just physically-linked
infrastructure) to companies headquartered in, or whose ultimate parent company is
headquartered in, Macau or a destination specified in Country Group D:5 in supplement no. 1 to
part 740 of the EAR. ‘Linked data centers’ are data centers that use networking infrastructure
that links two or more separate data centers together to allow physical and virtual facilities to
share resources, balance workloads, and replicate data as a unified whole. This prohibition is
described under § 744.24(a)(1) and the definition is provided in § 744.24(d). The term “for an
entity” as used in this § 744.24(a)(1) applies to instances in which there is “knowledge” that a
data center is providing remote access to a non-D:5 customer that is in turn training SI models
for, or participating in the training of SI models for, or providing SI models to a D:5 company.
This control will cut off additional potential diversion pathways and evasion efforts.

         These controls are necessary because providing computer power access at this level or
greater will allow for training or running of advanced SI models; the Secretary of Commerce has
determined that it could pose a national security and foreign policy threat to the United States
when such computing power access will be provided to or for companies headquartered, or
whose ultimate parent company is headquartered in, Macau or a destination specified in Country
Group D:5 – unless the computing power is exclusively used for commercial recommender
systems. Access to advanced computing power presents significant national security and foreign
policy concerns, primarily by enabling foreign countries or other entities of concern to accelerate
the development of weapons of mass destruction (WMD), execute sophisticated zero-day
cyberattacks on critical infrastructure, and disrupt global military balances of power – unless the
computing power is exclusively used for commercial recommender systems.

         Consistent with the national security and foreign policy concerns outlined in this IFR,
BIS is imposing a notification and authorization requirement for subsequent changes in end-use.
This requirement, provided in new paragraph (e) of § 744.24, and described in more detail
further in this section, is triggered if there is a change in end-use of the items described in
paragraph (a)(1) of § 744.24. In the event of such a change, a BIS authorization may be required.
Relatedly, Note 1 to paragraph (a)(1) of § 744.24 provides that the prohibition applies regardless
of whether the person in possession of the EAR item was a party to the transaction to the original
export, reexport, or transfer (in-country), i.e., the original shipment of the items to the data
center. Because this EAR end-use control under § 744.24(a)(1) applies to any subsequent
reexport or transfer (in-country), including changing from a non-prohibited end-use to a
prohibited end-use, it is important that parties in possession of these items are aware of the need
to review § 744.24(a)(1) for any subsequent reexport or transfer (in-country).

         The prohibition described in paragraph (a)(1) applies regardless of whether a U.S.-origin
item specified under paragraph (a) of § 744.24 has been incorporated or commingled into or with
a foreign-made item. Reexporters and transferors may not rely on the de minimis provisions for
incorporating or commingling U.S.-origin items specified under paragraph (a) of § 744.24 into
foreign-made items where there is “knowledge” at any point in the chain from the development,
production, or use of the foreign-made item that the U.S.-origin item will be used in this
prohibited end-use under paragraph (a)(1) of § 744.24. This prohibition under § 744.24 is
described under Note 2 to paragraph (a)(1).

         Note 3 to paragraph (a)(1) describes due diligence expectations imposed on “U.S.
persons” and foreign data centers to ensure compliance with these controls. Foreign data centers
will be expected to perform robust due diligence on their customers to identify direct or indirect
ownership or control by Country Group D:5 or Macau persons. This diligence will ensure that
companies cannot circumvent new controls through corporate restructurings or spinoffs.

         This IFR adds an “is informed” process under paragraph (a)(2) of § 744.24 to impose an
additional prohibition on persons informed by BIS that a license is required for the export,
reexport, or transfer (in-country) of specified items to certain persons or destinations, because
there is an unacceptable risk of use in, or diversion to, the activities specified in paragraph (a)(1)
of § 744.24. Notifications made under paragraph (a)(2) that there is an unacceptable risk of use
in, or diversion to an end use specified in § 744.24 may be made either individually by specific
notice or through amendment to the EAR.

         As described under § 744.24(b), no license exceptions may overcome the prohibition
described in paragraph (a)(2) of § 744.24, except when noted by BIS. The prohibitions under §
744.24 do not apply when the computing power access is being provided exclusively for a
‘commercial recommender system’ under the (b)(2) exception provided the reporting
requirements described therein are followed.

         Applications for licenses required by § 744.24 will be reviewed on a case-by-case basis
as described under § 744.24(c).

         This IFR adds paragraph (d) to define for purposes of § 744.24 the terms: ‘linked data
centers’; and ‘commercial recommender system’:

         For purposes of § 744.24, ‘linked data center’ means a data center that uses networking
infrastructure that links two or more separate data centers together that allows physical and
virtual facilities to share resources, balance workloads, and replicate data as a unified whole.
This includes digitally-linked infrastructure, not just physically-linked infrastructure.

         For purposes of § 744.24, ‘commercial recommender system’ means a Super Intelligence
(SI) tool exclusively designed and exclusively used to rank, filter, or suggest commercial
products, services, or content to users based on user behavior, transaction history, or item
attributes. This term does not include any SI system designed or used to generate novel text,
image, audio, video, software, or other content in response to user inputs, or any system that
incorporates such model, regardless of whether the system also performs recommendation
functions.

         This IFR adds paragraph (e) to impose a notification and authorization requirement for
subsequent changes in end-use. New paragraph (e) specifies that for any export, reexport, or
transfer made on or after [INSERT DATE OF PUBLICATION IN THE FEDERAL REGISTER]
without a license, i.e., because the exporter, reexporter, or transferor had no “knowledge” that the
item is for a prohibited end-use under this section and no other EAR license requirement applies,
the exporter, reexporter, or transferor must inform the recipient that BIS authorization is required
if the item will be transferred in-country; and that the recipient must follow the know-your-
customer guidance in supplement no. 3 Part 732 of the EAR. The notification also must specify
that the transferor, e.g., the data center operator, must obtain authorization for this change in end-
use. Changes in end-use from a non-prohibited end-use to a prohibited end-use under paragraphs
(a)(1) are considered a “transfer (in-country)” for purposes of paragraphs (a)(1) or (2) and
require an EAR authorization under § 744.24.
B. Conforming change to 744.23 for removal of worldwide license requirement in this IFR

         This IFR, as described under section II of this preamble, removes the worldwide license
requirement from former § 742.6(a)(6)(iii)(A). As a conforming change, this IFR makes
revisions to the advanced computing items end-use control under § 744.23(a)(3) to ensure that a
license will continue to be required for exports, reexports, and transfers (in-country) of these
advanced computing items when such items are destined for entities headquartered in, or whose
ultimate parent company is headquartered in, Country Group D:5 or Macau.

         Specifically, this IFR revises § 744.23 “Supercomputer,” “advanced-node integrated
circuits,” and semiconductor manufacturing equipment end-use controls under paragraph
(a)(3)(i)(A) to specify that this end-use control for advanced computing items includes all
advanced computing items (e.g., those specified in ECCNs 3A090 (except for 3A090.c), 4A090,
and related .z paragraph items). Prior to this IFR, there was an enforced license requirement
under § 742.6(a)(6)(iii)(A) of the EAR that applied to all destinations outside of the United
States for these advanced computing items when such items are destined to entities
headquartered in, or whose ultimate parent company is headquartered in, Country Group D:5 or
Macau. See the May 31, 2026, “Guidance Regarding Enforcement of License Requirements for
Advanced Computing Items for Entities Headquartered in Country Group D:5 and Macau” on
the BIS website at http://www.bis.gov. With the removal of the worldwide license requirement
this IFR makes a required conforming change to return the license requirement for these
advanced computing items to § 744.23(a)(3)(i)(A).

         For § 744.23(a)(3)(i)(A), BIS has received inquiries regarding the meaning of the phrase
“for an entity” that is headquartered in, or whose ultimate parent company is headquartered in,
either Macau or a destination specified in Country Group D:5. As stated in BIS’s October 2023
Advanced Computing/Supercomputing (“AC/S”) IFR (88 FR 73458) this control was intended to
ensure that the national security and foreign policy objectives of the October 7, 2022 IFR and the
AC/S IFR are not undermined by Macau, the People’s Republic of China (PRC), or other
Country Group D:5 headquartered entities setting up cloud servers or data centers in other
countries to allow these entities of concern to continue to train their SI models in ways that
would be contrary to U.S. national security and foreign policy interests. This end-use control
thus is not, and has never been limited only to exports, reexports, and transfers (in-country)
destined to such entities of concern as ultimate consignees or end users, but extends to exports,
reexports, or transfers (in-country) on behalf of these entities of concern, e.g., if an entity of
concern was the purchaser in the transaction (directly or indirectly) or was the exclusive or
primary (greater than 50 percent of total compute) remote end user of the data center computing
capacity. Exporters, reexporters, and transferors are encouraged to examine both end-use
controls in § 744.23(a)(3) and in new § 744.24 to the extent their transactions involve exports,
reexports, or transfers to data centers outside of the United States where there is knowledge that
a company headquartered in, or whose ultimate parent is headquartered in, Macau or D:5 will be
an intended remote end user. Any entities in violation of this requirement should submit a
voluntary self-disclosure (see Section 764.5 of the EAR). Bona fide operators of data centers
who are otherwise engaged in activities consistent with the EAR and were providing remote
access used for ‘commercial recommender systems’ (as defined in the new § 744.24(d)(1)) may
continue providing these services for 90 days, but must obtain a license following expiration of
this period to continue these services.Finally, § 744.23(a)(3)(i)(C) is added to create a license
requirement for “applicable advanced node integrated circuits” that are subject to the EAR,
specified in 3A090.a, designed by a company that is neither an approved IC designer (specified
in supplement no. 6 to part 740) or an authorized IC designer (as described in Note 1 to
3A090.a), and exported, exported from abroad, reexported, or transferred (in-country) by a “front
end fabricator” or “OSAT” to or within any destination. This addition ensures that a license
requirement continues to apply to “front end fabricators” and “OSATs” creating advanced ICs
for non-approved, non-authorized IC designers wherever located. Additionally, the presumption
created in Note 1 to 3A090.a continues to apply but can be overcome by the “front end
fabricator” or “OSAT” if they follow the due diligence steps specified in that note. BIS notes that
the inclusion of the phrase “exported from aboard” in this new paragraph is simply descriptive
and not intended to indicate that the activity of exporting from destinations other than the United
States is not captured in provisions of the EAR where that phrase is omitted.
IV. Rescinded Provisions of the AI Diffusion Rule
A. Revoking model weights controls

         This IFR revokes the controls on model weights published in the AI Diffusion Rule. BIS
has determined that a more effective approach to address national security and foreign policy
concerns that arise from the transfer of model weights is through the use of specified destination-
based or end-use license requirements. Accordingly, ECCN 4E091 is removed from the CCL in
supplement no. 1 to part 774. This IFR also makes the necessary conforming changes to other
parts of the EAR to remove references to ECCN 4E091 and model weights by: (1) removing Red
Flag 28 in supplement no. 3 to part 732; (2) removing the AI Model weights Foreign Direct
Product (FDP) rule in § 734.9; (3) removing reference to 4E091 from § 740.2; (4) removing the
RS license requirement text that applies to AI model weights in § 742.6; (5) removing 748.3(f);
and (6) removing the definition of “Model Weights” in § 772.1.
B. Removing country tiers, Data Center VEUs, and related regulatory language

         This IFR revokes the tiers of countries created in the AI Diffusion Rule and the final rule
for “Expansion of Validated End User Authorization: Data Center Validated End User
Authorization,” 89 FR 80080 (Oct. 2, 2024), which were implemented through License
Exception Artificial Intelligence Authorization (AIA) in § 740.27, supplement no. 5 to part 740,
the Data Center Validated End-Users (VEU) Authorization in § 748.15 and supplement no. 8 to
part 748 (including reverting the reference to General VEU Authorization to use VEU
Authorization wherever the term is used).

         Specifically, this IFR removes License Exception AIA from the EAR by: (1) removing
reference to this license exception in § 740.2; (2) reserving § 740.27 for future use; (3) removing
supplement no. 5 to part 740; and (4) removing AIA from ECCNs in supplement no. 1 to part
774 when listed in the list-based license exceptions section. This IFR removes the Data Center
VEU Authorization from § 748.15, supplement no. 8 to part 748, and supplement no. 10 to part
748. This IFR also makes conforming changes to the license review policy in § 742.6 to remove
references to supplement no. 5 to part 740.
C. Conforming changes due to the recission of the AI Diffusion Rule

         1) Removing License Exceptions AIA from § 740.2 Restrictions on all License
Exceptions;

         2) Removing License Exceptions Artificial Intelligence Authorization (AIA) (§
         740.27) and License Exception Low Processing Performance (LPP) (§ 740.29) from the
         EAR;
         3) Removing references to the revoked License Exceptions AIA and LPP (formerly in §
740.27 and § 740.29) and adding the notification and authorization requirements in the new §
744.24 in § 762.2 Records to be retained; and
         4) Removing references to the revoked License Exceptions AIA, and LPP (formerly in §
740.27 and § 740.29) from the CCL.
D. Foreign-Direct Product Rules
         BIS is retaining the worldwide destination scope of the advanced computing foreign
direct product (FDP) rule in § 734.9(h)(2)(i) if there is “knowledge” that a foreign-produced item
is destined worldwide or will be incorporated into any “part,” “component,” “computer,” or
“equipment” not designated EAR99 to any location worldwide. Retention of this provision
ensures the prohibitions of relevant controls are not otherwise limited.
         Though BIS is not making any changes to § 734.9(h), BIS notes that the agency has
received questions about the application of that paragraph. Consistent with the robust controls
implemented in this IFR, BIS notes that provision’s expansive application. If a foreign-produced
product is subject to the EAR pursuant to § 734.9(h), BIS retains jurisdiction of that item after its
incorporation into another foreign-produced item, as provided by the text of § 734.9(h)(2)(i). For
example, if an ECCN 3A090 IC is incorporated into a foreign-produced “part,” “component,”
“computer,” or “equipment” (such as a GPU board or server), BIS continues to have jurisdiction
over the IC and license requirements may apply to the export, reexport, or transfer (in-country)
of the IC (and by extension, the server). Generally, BIS does not retain jurisdiction over items
after incorporation into other items, absent the applicability of the EAR’s de minimis provisions.
But in this context, specifically, in the relevant ECCN entries (ECCN 4A090 and those ECCNs
with a .z paragraph), the CCL notes that such items only meet the specifications for those
ECCNs if they contain an ECCN 3A090 IC. Thus, even third parties who only receive a 4A090
item are on notice that such items contain an ECCN 3A090 IC. Furthermore, the retention of
BIS’s jurisdiction under § 734.9(h) after incorporation is reflected by past BIS guidance to
inform parties in the supply chain of the applicability of the FDP rule, including the Model
Certification for Purposes of the FDP Rule of supplement no. 1 to part 734 of the EAR.

         In addition, consistent with the removal of ECCN 4E091, this IFR removes the AI Model
weights FDP rule in § 734.9(l).
E. License Exception Advanced Compute Manufacturing ACM

         BIS is retaining License Exception ACM under § 740.28 due to the benefits this license
exception plays in supporting the supply chains of export, reexports, and transfers (in-country) of
advanced SI items destined to ultimate end users outside of Macau or destinations in Country
Group D:5.
V. Amendments to the Presumptions Faced by Front-End Fabricators and OSAT
companies

         This IFR also changes the presumptions faced by front-end fabricators and OSAT
companies defined in Note 1 to 3A090.a. Specifically, paragraphs b. and c. of Note 1 to 3A090.a
are amended to remove eligibility to overcome the presumption in Note 1 to 3A090.a for an
integrated circuit designer located in or having an ultimate parent headquartered in a destination
specified in Country Groups D;1, D:4, or D:5 of supplement no. 1 to part 740 of the EAR or in
paragraph (a)(6)(iii)(A)(1) of § 742.6. Such companies which are not Authorized or Approved IC
designers and face a presumption that their IC is 3A090.a for all “applicable advanced logic
integrated circuits”. This addresses potential diversion pathways of advanced ICs.

VI. Conforming Changes
         In addition to the changes described in Sections II and III, this IFR makes conforming

changes to the EAR due to the changes described in this preamble.
A. Revisions to part 743

         For a transaction to be excluded from the license requirements of § 742.6(a)(6)(iii)(1), the
exporter, reexporter, or transferor must comply with the reporting requirement introduced by this
IFR in new § 743.10 Reporting Requirements to Qualify for Exclusion from Certain Regional
Stability Requirements. The reporting requirement mitigates the risk that transactions excluded
from this license requirement will be used or diverted contrary to the EAR by allowing the U.S.
Government to monitor the items being exported, reexported, or transferred (in-country).

         New § 743.10 describes in paragraph (b) the information to be included in the report; in
paragraph (c), where to submit the report; in paragraph (d), the timeframe as to when reporting is
required; and in paragraph (e), contacts for general information or questions about this report.
B. Addition to part 772

         In addition to removing the definition for “model weights” described in section IV.A. of
this preamble, this IFR adds a definition to §772.1 for “Aggregate TPP” to describe the sum TPP
of a group of ICs or commodities.
C. Revisions to the CCL

         Due to the revisions to the RS reason for control under § 742.6 made in this IFR,
conforming revisions are made to ECCNs: 3A001, 3A090, 3D001, 3E001, 4A003, 4A004,
4A005, 4A090, 4D001, 4D090, 4E001, 5A002, 5A992, 5A004, 5D002, 5E002, 5E992, and
5D992. For these ECCNs, the reason for control table is revised by removing references to items
paragraphs 3A090.a and .b, 4A090.a and .b, and the corresponding .z paragraphs. Additionally,
the reference to the worldwide RS license requirement in the control tables is revised with a new
reference that a license is required to or within any destination specified in §742.6(a)(6)(iii)(A)
of the EAR.

Export Control Reform Act of 2018

         On August 13, 2018, the President signed into law the John S. McCain National Defense
Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2019, which included the Export Control Reform Act of 2018
(ECRA), 50 U.S.C. sections 4801–4852. ECRA, as amended, provides the legal basis for BIS’s
principal authorities and serves as the authority under which BIS issues this IFR. In particular,
and as noted elsewhere, Section 1753 of ECRA (50 U.S.C. Section 4812) authorizes the
regulation of exports, reexports, and transfers (in-country) of items subject to U.S. jurisdiction.
Further, Section 1754(a)(1)-(16) of ECRA (50 U.S.C. Section 4813(a)(1)-(16)) authorizes, inter
alia, the establishment of a list of controlled items; the prohibition of unauthorized exports,
reexports, and transfers (in-country); the requirement of licenses or other authorizations for
exports, reexports, and transfers (in-country) of controlled items; apprising the public of changes
in policy, regulations, and procedures; and any other action necessary to carry out ECRA that is
not otherwise prohibited by law. Pursuant to Section 1762(a) of ECRA (50 U.S.C. Section
4821(a)), these changes can be imposed in an IFR without prior notice and comment.
