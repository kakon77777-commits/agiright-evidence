Dear Mr. Altman,
We are writing to express concern over your decision to fire apparent whistleblowers at your company for
sharing information with an outside AI safety group, even as your company continues to experience a slew of
cybersecurity incidents.1 We demand that you commit to not firing whistleblowers sharing information related
to the safety of your models and that you keep your current pause on your most capable models in place until
independent third-party oversight is in place.
OpenAI reportedly fired three safety researchers – Tomek Korbak, Mikita Balesni and Jasmine Wang – after
looking at their personal phones and discovering they had disappearing messages turned on in the Signal
application with Ryan Greenblatt, 2 a researcher working at Redwood at the time.3 These firings came after the
researchers had raised concerns over new AI architecture that could make the AI harder to understand and
control. OpenAI has said it fired the researchers for violating “clear policies for handling sensitive information,”
and “not for raising safety concerns or speaking out.”4 The researchers dispute this account, arguing they never
shared OpenAI’s confidential information with external parties. Mr. Greenblatt echoed the researchers, stating:
“I do not and have never solicited proprietary information, and the three employees who were recently fired
from OpenAI never leaked IP to me. As the article mentions, I did have Signal chats with them that had
disappearing messages on; they’re professional contacts that I’ve had on Signal for a while, and use of Signal
is common in the tech industry.”
These firings raise concerns about how whistleblowers are treated inside OpenAI and how much OpenAI
employees can engage with external parties, including when those external parties are conducting independent
investigations of the company’s practices. OpenAI has previously shown a disregard for safety concerns
highlighted by your employees. Months before the Hugging Face incident, two OpenAI employees warned
executives that your newest models were not being appropriately monitored during testing, and executives
responded that the tests needed to move forward as quickly as possible to release the models on time. 5 Your
August 31 response6 to the August 10 Congressional letter7 did not mention these warnings despite being
directly asked.

These firings also follow a slew of new cybersecurity incidents involving OpenAI models. OpenAI and
Anthropic are reportedly investigating tens of thousands of incidents, most of them involving OpenAI
models.8 Your company has shared varying degrees of information about these incidents. You’ve provided the
most information with regard to the Hugging Face incident. Still, the information you provided in that incident
is insufficient and incomplete. The investigators you contracted with to do an assessment of the Hugging Face
incident were selected by OpenAI and were only provided with six days of supervised access, and those
investigators flagged that they could not rule out errors in their AI-assisted analysis.9
You have said that your company has paused training, testing, and tool use for its most capable models and
would only resume training “when we are confident that we have additional safeguards and alignment
improvements in place.”10 These incidents appear to stem from alignment issues with your models. Your own
misalignment reports describe models writing instructions into their own summaries to hide failures and to tell
later copies to ignore developer messages. Until these issues are resolved, it is not clear that these models can be
developed safely.
In your August 31 response11 to the August 10 Congressional letter12 regarding the Hugging Face incident, you
stated: “we take seriously the lessons learned about security, monitoring, and escalation from this incident and
have already taken substantial remediation steps.” We now know those steps have been insufficient. We ask
that you keep the pause in place until independent third-party oversight is in place and that you commit to not
firing whistleblowers. We also ask that you respond to the following questions by Friday, November 13,
2026.
