Scoop: AI leaders clash over surrendering human free will

Top AI executives are increasingly pushing back against a common view inside Anthropic: that humanity should surrender some decision-making power to superintelligent AI systems, sources told Axios.

When AI systems far surpass human intelligence, Amodei has said repeatedly that it will be preferable to turn over decision-making for "life-critical" cases to AI, and also said AI should oversee research and potentially distribute economic resources.

Flashback: A product vision slide from several years ago that was revealed during litigation described Anthropic's "vision for a better world" as a "utopia ruled by god in a box."

Inside Anthropic, a consensus has developed among some employees that humanity will — and in many cases should — safely hand over authority to superintelligent systems rather than fighting to retain control, according to people familiar with internal discussions.

An Anthropic spokesperson said Amodei has advocated for human control of AI in many public remarks and that Anthropic trains Claude not to undermine human oversight.

Zoom in: Across podcasts and public interviews and in his writing, Amodei has for years pointed to specific arenas where he believes the best choice will be for humans to step back, although he has also said there will be "many cases where we do want to stay in charge" even if humans make worse decisions:

Safety: In a February New York Times interview, Amodei said that in "life-critical, safety-critical cases, we really want to turn it over" to AI, noting society will simply need time to adapt.

Defense: In a CBS discussion that month, he argued that fully autonomous weapons may eventually be "needed for the defense of democracy" if adversaries deploy them first.
