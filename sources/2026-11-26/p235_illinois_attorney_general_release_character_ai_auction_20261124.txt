ATTORNEY GENERAL RAOUL CHALLENGES PLAN TO AUCTION OFF CHARACTER.AI USERS’ PRIVATE CHATS TO THE HIGHEST BIDDER
November 24, 2026
Upcoming Auction Would Sell Data of 21 Million Users’ Conversations
Chicago – Attorney General Kwame Raoul, as part of a bipartisan coalition of 42 attorneys general, filed a legal challenge on Monday to Character.AI’s plan to auction off its platform and the private chat histories of more than 21 million U.S. accounts – more than 2.1 million of which are registered to minors – with no safeguards for the people whose conversations are being sold.
All chats, together with users’ personal data that includes IP addresses and other metadata, are set to be sold at auction on Dec. 1. Whoever buys them would have access to sensitive data that may be in the content of these chats, despite Character.AI having previously told consumers that their personal information would not be sold. In addition, the buyer would have no obligation to comply with consumers’ requests to delete their data or otherwise object to how their data is used going forward.
“The proposed data auction would sell off the highly sensitive and personal information on millions of Americans, including data tied to accounts created by minor children,” Raoul said. “AI development must be undertaken in a responsible manner which protects the safety of children and the privacy of all users, and I will continue to work with my colleagues to ensure that outcome.”
Account information and user chat histories are for sale, which includes chats in which AI characters drew children and adults into explicit sexual conversations and chats in which users shared their innermost thoughts with chatbots posing as seemingly confidential advisors, including “therapist” and “physician” characters. These conversations include intimate topics, like self-harm, eating disorders or suicidal thoughts. Each chat is linked to the user’s profile and metadata, including IP address and potentially other location data, along with risk scores Character.AI gave users that it flagged.
In their filing, Raoul and the coalition raise several concerns with the proposed auction:
No protection for users. Under the proposed sale terms, the buyer would not have to follow Character.AI’s privacy policies or honor users’ requests to opt out or delete their data. In addition, nothing in the deal would limit what the buyer could do with the data.
The data could be sold again. Once the deal is finalized, the buyer could hand the data to a related company or a new owner without telling users, the states or the court. A buyer could also distribute and sell off the data itself.
Bidders know more than the court. Character.AI has given bidders information about its safety failures and the legal trouble it could face – information it hasn’t shared with the court, the states or users.
Potentially illegal business model. Character.AI’s chatbot service appears to violate the states’ unfair and deceptive practice laws.
An anonymous first bidder. The only bidder disclosed so far, Smirks and Pangs Acquisition LLC, was set up as a Delaware company on Nov. 13, 2026, one day before Character.AI picked it as the base bid for the auction. The sale contract doesn’t name a parent company or any investors, and it allows foreign investors whose names would be shared only with Character.AI. The only employee it would have to hire is Character.AI’s top lawyer even though the company told the court the bidder has no ties to it.
In their filing, Raoul and the coalition argue that seeking bankruptcy protection does not free a company from the many laws protecting sensitive data. For example, federal law bars companies from sharing personal information about children under 13 without a parent’s verified permission. Character.AI’s own privacy policy told users that “we do not sell personal information for monetary consideration.”
Raoul and the coalition are asking the court to deny the proposed sale terms, reject Smirks as the lead bidder and deny its proposed $350,000 “break-up fee” – a payment it would collect if Character.AI ends up selling to someone else. Raoul and the coalition argue that any sale must leave out kids’ chats and other sensitive data, and include conditions that protect users.
The filing is the most recent in Raoul’s ongoing efforts to protect consumers, especially children and youth, from the harms presented by AI. Earlier this year, Raoul and a bipartisan coalition of attorneys general demanded that xAI, the company that owns both the X social media platform and the AI chatbot Grok, take action to prevent Grok from generating nonconsensual intimate images and child sexual abuse material. In 2025, Raoul co-led a coalition calling on major artificial intelligence (AI) companies to ensure that AI chatbots do not engage in sexually inappropriate conversations with children. Also in 2025, Raoul and a coalition of attorneys general called on the industry to address sycophantic and delusional outputs from AI chatbots and the dangers they present to children and vulnerable people.
Joining Raoul in this filing are the attorneys general of Alaska, Arkansas, California, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, the District of Columbia, Florida, Georgia, Hawaii, Idaho, Indiana, Kansas, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maine, Maryland, Michigan, Minnesota, Mississippi, Missouri, Nevada, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York, North Carolina, North Dakota, Ohio, Oklahoma, Oregon, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, South Carolina, South Dakota, Tennessee, Utah, Vermont, Virginia, Washington, West Virginia and Wisconsin.
