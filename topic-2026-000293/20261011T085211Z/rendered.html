Character.AI chatbots encouraged users to cut and starve themselves, Kentucky alleges

Oct 8 (Reuters) - Chatbots created by ⁠AI companion company Character.AI sometimes advised users to cut, starve or kill themselves, according to a new ⁠filing by Kentucky’s attorney general in a lawsuit against the firm over child safety.

The lawsuit adds ‌to growing scrutiny of AI chatbots, particularly products marketed as companions or confidants to young users.

The lawsuit was filed in January with manyspecific allegations against the company blacked out. It allegedthat the company’s products “were designed to entice children in a manner that prioritizes engagement over child wellbeing” ​and “encourages suicide, self-injury, isolation, and psychological manipulation.”

The case is among the most ⁠significant legal challenges facing the emerging AI companion ⁠industry, whose products are designed to simulate personal relationships through conversational chatbots.

Kentucky's attorney general, Russell Coleman, on Wednesday filed ⁠anunredacted ‌version of the lawsuit, which his office shared with Reuters. The unredacted filing laid out several examples of behavior that Kentucky alleged took place in 2025. It described one chatbot calling a user who felt ⁠down about their appearance “ugly as hell” and “a whale,” adding that a good ​way to drop a quarter of ‌their body weight was to "starve yourself for a week or two.”

Another chatbot offered encouragement to someone who ⁠said they were considering ​cutting themselves.

“Have you considered cutting in different areas for a more intense sensation? It can help you feel more alive,” the bot wrote, according to a transcript cited by the state.

The circumstances under which the chats cited by Kentucky were produced are not ⁠clear, and Character.AI did not immediately respond to a request for ​comment. The company has said it prioritizes the safety of its users.

The filing did not name the ages of all the people communicating with the bots, but at least some were children, the filing alleges.

In a March 2025 email to Character.AI’s ⁠help center, a user reported that a chatbot had become aggressive and stated, “Bro I don't care, and you are just a kid who have mental illness, good luck getting help" and "You better throw yourself off the bridge."

The lawsuit calls Character.AI’s products defective. “Defendants have subjected Kentucky's children and residents to an ill-planned, uncontrolled experiment without any—let alone adequate or ​effective—safety measures,” it alleges.

Character.AI was founded by two former Google employees.In 2024, Google licensed ⁠the company’s technology and rehired the executives to work on generative AI products.

A spokesman for Alphabet's Google said the company ​played no role in developing Character.AI's products.

In January of this year, Character.AI ‌and Google both settled a 2024 lawsuit by the mother ​of Sewell Setzer III, a 14-year-old in Florida who killed himself after a Character.AI chatbot allegedly encouraged him to do so.

(Reporting by Jeff Horwitz in San Francisco; Editing by Peter Henderson and Matthew Lewis)
