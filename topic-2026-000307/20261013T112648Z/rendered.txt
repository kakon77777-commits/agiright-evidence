Special meeting of the AI Scientific Panel
Special meeting of the AI Scientific Panel
NEWS ARTICLE
Publication 12 October 2026

On 9 October 2026, Henna Virkkunen, Executive Vice-President for Tech Sovereignty, Security and Democracy, and Seán Ó hÉigeartaigh, Chair of the AI Scientific Panel, convened a special meeting.

The meeting of the AI Scientific Panel aimed to inform the Commission's actions, in view of recent incidents of loss of control, and technical developments related to monitorability of models and recursive self-improvement. The chair also presented key recommendations for the work of the AI Office.

Recent loss-of-control incidents

The panel has been investigating recent loss-of-control incidents. The chair and vice-chair presented what is known about the incidents to date and highlighted what we still do not know. The panel has worked together with the AI Office on a set of questions for the companies that developed the models involved. The AI Office has already requested and received serious incident reports and sent requests for information on security practices. Building on these efforts, the Commission will send requests for information with the combined questions of the panel and the AI Office to further investigate these incidents.

State of play of technical developments

The chair presented evidence related to the possibility of AI recursive self-improvement and related risks. The panel also discussed recent developments in training techniques which reduce the monitorability of models and its implications for future incidents and risk mitigation.

Chair's Recommendations

The chair and vice-chair of the panel presented key recommendations to the Executive Vice-President and the AI Office in relation to frontier AI safety and security. In particular, they recommend to:

ensure governance of the risk posed by internal models during development activities and deployment for testing;
strengthen EU capabilities related to detection, evaluation and investigation of incidents of this nature (e.g., via a task force, preparing the technical and procedural groundwork for future investigations);
establish an international initiative to facilitate information-sharing and common requirements for frontier AI developers, as well as joint crisis response and prevention;
ensure enhanced scrutiny of training techniques and architectures that reduce monitorability, given the risks they present.
